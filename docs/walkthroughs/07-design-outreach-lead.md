# ⚠️ Before you start — read this once

Your account can **open** all of your screens, but at the moment it may not be able to **save** on them. The system keeps "which pages you can see" and "what you are allowed to change" as two separate settings, and the second one has not been switched on for your role yet.

**What you will see if it has not been:**

| Where | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Catalog**, **Brands**, **Assets** | The pages open and you can read everything. Clicking **Save Changes** shows a red message reading **"Forbidden resource"** or *"Something went wrong. Refresh the page or try again in a moment."* |
| **Experiences** | The page shows **"Couldn't load events. Refresh the page to try again."** and stays empty however many times you refresh. |
| **Promotions** | The page shows **"Could not load coupons"** with a **Try again** button that keeps failing. |

**None of this is something you did wrong, and refreshing will not fix it.**

> 💡 **The fix takes the Founder/Admin about thirty seconds.** Send them this exact sentence: *"Please grant the Design/Outreach Lead role the **Manage operations** permission on the Permissions screen — I can see Catalog, Experiences, Brands, Assets and Promotions but cannot save on any of them."*

Everything below describes how each screen works once that is switched on. Read it now, and it will all be waiting for you.

# 🍽️ Catalog

Sidebar → **Catalog**. The page title is **Menu**.

This is where every product lives. It is organised as **brand → category → product**.

## Finding your way around

1. **Brand tabs** run across the top — one per active food brand. The seeded ones are **Konma Food** and **Just Craves**. Click a tab to see that brand's menu.
2. Under the tabs, products are grouped into **categories**. Each category header shows its name and a count, like `3 items`.
3. Each product is a card showing its name, its price (e.g. `₹480`), how many variants it has, **how many images it has**, and a **Published** switch.

> ⚠️ A card that says **"No images"** shows it in a warning colour. That is the system telling you a customer will see a grey placeholder tile instead of a photo. Those are your first jobs.

**Empty states you might meet:**

- **"No Active Food Brands"** — *"Create an active food brand in the Brands page to manage its menu."* with a **Go to Brands** button.
- **"No Categories Yet"** — *"Create a category for this brand to start organising products."*
- **"No items in this category."**
- If something fails to load: **"Could not load the menu"** — *"Brands, categories or products failed to load."* with **Try again**.

## Categories

1. Click **Add Category** (top right) or **Add Category** in an empty-brand state.
2. **Name** — required. The hint suggests *"e.g. Starters"*.
3. **Sort Order (optional)** — a number. Lower numbers appear higher up the menu. The seeded categories run **Signature Plates (10)**, **Beverages (20)**, **Pantry & Provisions (30)**, **Experiences (40)**, **Villa Merchandise (50)** — leaving gaps of ten so you can slot something new in between without renumbering everything.
4. Click **Add Category**. You will see **"Category created."**

## Writing product copy

1. Click **Edit** on a product card (or **Add Item** in a category header for a new one). A panel slides in from the right.
2. It has three tabs: **Details**, **Variants** and **Media**. Copy lives in **Details**.

| Field | What to put in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | What the product is called. Hint: *"e.g. Sourdough Loaf"*. Required. |
| **Slug** | The web address. Helper text: *"Used in the storefront URL. Lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens."* It fills itself in from the Name — leave it alone unless you have a reason. |
| **Type** | **Prepared food**, **Packaged**, **Experience** or **Merchandise**. |
| **Category** | Which section of the menu it sits in. |
| **Base Price (INR)** | The price in rupees. Required. |
| **Description (optional)** | **Your storefront copy.** The hint reads *"Short storefront description"*. |
| **Published** | On means customers can see it. Off means it is a draft. |

3. Click **Save Changes**. You will see **"Product updated."**

> 💡 **The Description is the single most valuable box on this screen.** It is what a customer reads under the product name on the live page, *and* it is what appears in Google results and in the preview card when someone shares the link on WhatsApp. It is one line, so make it earn its place. Compare the seeded copy: *"A live culture, feed flour, a jar and a feeding card. Everything to bake by Sunday."* — concrete, specific, and it tells you what you get.

> ⚠️ The Description is a **single-line box**, not a paragraph box. Write one strong sentence or two short ones.

## Publishing and unpublishing

There is no separate publish button. The **Published** switch *is* it, and it appears in two places:

- On the product card in the menu grid — flick it and it saves immediately. You will see **"Product published."** or **"Product unpublished."**
- Inside the edit panel, saved along with everything else.

**Published means:** the product appears on the public storefront and can be bought. **Unpublished (draft) means:** it exists for the team only, and the public page for it will not resolve.

> 💡 Write the copy and add the photo **before** you publish. A live page with a placeholder tile is worse than no page.

## Photos

Open a product → the **Media** tab. The heading is **Media**, and the helper line tells you the rule that matters most:

> *"The first image is the one the storefront card, the product page and the share preview lead with. Drag, or use the arrows, to change which that is."*

### Uploading a photo

1. In the drop area — **"Drop an image, or pick one"**, **"JPEG, PNG or WebP · up to 10 MB"** — either drag a file in or click **Choose image**.
2. The file name appears with its type and size.
3. Fill in **Alt text (required)**. The hint suggests *"Sourdough loaf, sliced, on a wooden board"* and the helper explains: *"Describe the photo. The storefront gallery, the product card and the share preview all read this."*
4. Click **Upload image**. You will see it work through *"Requesting an upload slot..."*, *"Uploading... 60%"*, *"Linking it to the product..."*, then **"Image added."**

**Limits, exactly:** JPEG, PNG or WebP only, maximum 10 MB. Anything else is refused before it uploads, with a message naming the problem — for example *"beach.gif is a image/gif file. Product media must be JPEG, PNG or WebP."* or *"hero.jpg is 14.2 MB. The limit is 10 MB."*

### Why alt text is required

You cannot upload without it — the **Upload image** button stays greyed out until the box has something in it, and trying anyway says:

> *"Alt text is required — it is what the storefront gallery and the OG card read out."*

Editing an existing image's alt text and clearing it gives:

> *"Alt text is required — the storefront gallery and the OG card both read it."*

**Three real reasons it matters:**

1. **A guest using a screen reader hears it instead of seeing the photo.** Without it they get nothing — a silent gap where your best image should be. That is a person shut out of the page.
2. **Google reads it.** It is a large part of how a photo of your thali gets found by someone searching for one.
3. **It is the share preview.** When someone sends the link in WhatsApp, the alt text is what the preview card reads from.

**How to write one:** describe what is actually in the frame, plainly, in about ten words. Look at the seeded examples — *"Chef's table counter set for a tasting menu"*, *"Two Konma ceramic mugs in terracotta and olive glaze"*, *"Cross-back linen apron hanging on a hook"*. Do not write "photo of" or "image of", and do not repeat the product name back at itself.

### Which image leads

**The first image in the list is the cover.** It is tagged with a badge reading **Cover & OG image**, and it is the one used on the menu card, at the top of the product page, and in the share preview.

There is **no "make this the cover" button** — you change the cover by changing the order:

1. Drag a row to the top, **or** use the up and down arrows on the row.
2. It saves as you go: **"Gallery order saved."**

New uploads always go to the **end** of the list, so uploading a better photo does not promote it. You must move it up.

### Removing a photo

1. Click the bin icon on a row.
2. A confirmation appears. If you are deleting the cover it warns you: *"This is the cover image. Removing it promotes the next one — or leaves the storefront on its placeholder tile if there is no next one. This cannot be undone."*
3. Click **Remove image**. You will see **"Image removed."**

With no images at all you get **"No images yet"** — *"The storefront falls back to a placeholder tile until this product has at least one image."*

# 🏷️ Brands

Sidebar → **Brands**. The page title is **Brands**.

A brand is the identity a product or experience belongs to. The seeded ones are **Konma Food** and **Just Craves**.

## Reading the page

Each brand is a card showing its name, a **type** badge — **Food**, **Art** or **Lifestyle** — a **status** badge, and its owner (or **No owner**).

Filter with the status tabs — **All**, **Idea**, **Planning**, **Development**, **Active**, **Paused** — or the search box (*"Search brands..."*).

Empty state: **"No Brands Yet"** — *"Add your food, art, or lifestyle brands to get started."*

## The fields

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | The brand name. Hint: *"e.g. Konma Kitchen"*. Required. |
| **Brand Type** | **Food**, **Art** or **Lifestyle** |
| **Status** | **Idea** → **Planning** → **Development** → **Active** → **Paused** |
| **Notes (optional)** | *"Additional context about this brand..."* |

> ⚠️ **Only a brand set to Active and typed Food gets a tab on the Catalog page.** If a brand's menu has vanished, check its status here first.

> 💡 **Adding and deleting brands is Founder/Admin only** — you will not see an **Add Brand** button or a bin icon. You will see **Edit** on brands you personally own. That is deliberate: brands are the top of the tree and everything hangs off them.

# 📁 Assets

Sidebar → **Assets**. The page title is **Assets**.

This is the team's internal file cupboard — **not** customer-facing. Recipes, standard operating procedures, menus, cost sheets and training documents.

## Reading the page

A table with the columns **Name**, **Type**, **Brand**, **Uploaded by**, **Date**, **Status**.

Filter with the status tabs — **All**, **Draft**, **In Review**, **Approved**, **Rejected** — the type dropdown (**All types**, then **Recipe**, **SOP**, **Menu**, **Cost Sheet**, **Training Doc**), or the search box (*"Search assets..."*).

An approved asset carries an extra chip reading **Ready for display**.

Empty state: **"No assets yet"** — *"Upload recipes, SOPs, menus, or training docs for your team."*

## Uploading

1. Click **Upload Asset**.
2. **Name** — required. Hint: *"e.g. Nasi Goreng Recipe v2"*. Put a version number in it; you will thank yourself.
3. **Asset Type** — **Recipe**, **SOP**, **Menu**, **Cost Sheet** or **Training Doc**.
4. **Brand (optional)** — which brand it belongs to.
5. Drop your file in the box — **"Drop files here, or click to browse"**, **"Photos, documents, video up to 10 MB"**. It accepts more than the Catalog does: JPEG, PNG, WebP, PDF, Word documents, MP4 and WebM.
6. Click **Upload Asset**. You will see **"Asset uploaded."**

If the file is the wrong type or too big: *"Upload failed. Check your file type and size (max 10 MB) and try again."*

## The review flow

Assets move **Draft → In Review → Approved** (or **Rejected**).

1. Open an asset you created and set **Status** to **In Review** when it is ready for someone to check.
2. Saving shows a message matching what you did — **"Asset submitted for review."**, **"Asset approved."**, **"Asset rejected."** or **"Asset updated."**

> 💡 You can only edit assets **you uploaded**. Approving and rejecting belongs to whoever holds operations permissions.

> ⚠️ Missing a required field gives *"Please fill in all required fields before saving."*

# 🌿 Experiences

Sidebar → **Experiences**. The page title is **Experience Events**.

An experience is something that happens **on a date, for a fixed number of people** — a dinner, a workshop, a tasting. The two seeded ones are **Chef's Table Dinner** (dining, 12 seats, ₹4,500) and **Fermentation Workshop** (workshop, 16 seats, ₹2,500).

## Reading the page

A table with the columns **Title**, **Date**, **Type**, **Status**, **Capacity**, **Bookings** and actions.

- **Type** badges: **Dining**, **Workshop**, **Pop-Up**, **Tasting**, **Other**
- **Status** badges: **Draft**, **Upcoming**, **Live**, **Past**, **Cancelled** — *the system works these out from the date; you do not set them*
- **Capacity** shows `4 / 12` with a fill bar and either `8 left` or **Full**. Hovering explains what counts: *"Seats taken: confirmed + attended + checkout holds that have not expired."*

Empty state: **"No Events Created"** — *"Create an event to start collecting bookings."*

## Creating an experience

1. Click **Create Event**.
2. **Title** — hint *"e.g. Sunset Dinner Experience"*. At least 3 characters, or you get *"Title must be at least 3 characters"*.
3. **Event Type** — **Dining**, **Workshop**, **Pop-Up**, **Tasting** or **Other**.
4. **Date & Time** — pick the date and the start time.
5. **Capacity** — how many people. Minimum 1.
6. **Price** — in rupees.
7. **Zone (optional)** — where in the villa. The seeded zones include **Dining Hall**, **Garden Terrace** and **Workshop Studio**.
8. **Brand (optional)** — which brand runs it.
9. **Description (optional)** — **this is the copy customers read.** Up to 2000 characters, so unlike a product description you have room for a proper paragraph.
10. **Image URL (optional)** — a web address for the picture. *Experiences do not have the drag-and-drop uploader that products do — you paste a link here.*
11. Click **Create Event**. You will see **"Event created."**

> 💡 Read the seeded descriptions before writing your own — *"Twelve seats, one counter, an eight-course tasting cooked in front of you."* and *"A hands-on afternoon on koji, kanji and sourdough. Take your starter home."* Both name the number, the thing, and what you walk away with. Copy that shape.

## Editing and deleting

1. Click the pencil icon on a row. The panel opens titled **Edit Event**.
2. Change what you need and click **Save Changes** → **"Event updated."**

The bin icon deletes. It warns you first: *"This will permanently remove the event and all its bookings. This cannot be undone."* with **Keep Event** and **Delete Event**.

> ⚠️ **Deleting an experience deletes its bookings too.** If people have booked, cancel or reschedule — do not delete.

## Bookings and attendance

Click the **Bookings** button on a row (its tooltip reads *"Bookings, held seats and attendance"*).

The panel shows seats taken out of capacity, then two sections:

**Held by a checkout in progress** — seats someone has in their basket right now, with a countdown like `Releases in 04:12`. When empty: *"Nothing is held right now. A hold appears the moment a customer quotes this experience."*

**Bookings** — the guest list, with columns **Guest**, **Party**, **Payment**, **Status**, **Attendance**. Statuses are **Held**, **Confirmed**, **Cancelled**, **Attended**, **No Show**; payment shows **Pending**, **Paid**, **Refunded** or **Free**.

On the day, each confirmed guest gets **Attended** and **No-show** buttons. Before the event starts they are greyed out (*"Attendance opens when the experience starts."*).

> ⚠️ **Mark your no-shows before you use "Mark all attended".** The confirmation says so plainly: *"Mark your no-shows first — an attended booking cannot be changed back."* Marking someone attended opens their review invitation and credits loyalty on their order.

# 🎟️ Promotions

Sidebar → **Promotions**. The page title is **Promotions**, subtitled *"Coupon codes customers can apply at checkout."*

## Creating a coupon

1. Click **New coupon**. The panel says *"A new coupon starts as a draft — nothing is offered to customers until you set it to Active."*
2. **Code** — what the customer types, e.g. `WELCOME10`. 3 to 32 characters. It is saved in capitals whatever you type: *"Saved upper-cased, so `welcome10` and `WELCOME10` are the same coupon."*
3. **Status** — **Draft**, **Active** or **Disabled**. Leave it on **Draft** while you set it up.
4. **Description (optional)** — an internal note. *"Internal note — what this offer is for."* Customers never see it.
5. **Discount type** — **Percentage off**, **Flat amount off**, or **Free shipping**.
6. Then, depending on the type:
   - **Percentage off** → a percentage box, plus **Maximum discount (₹)** with the hint *"No ceiling"*. **Always set a ceiling on a percentage coupon** — 20% off a ₹4,500 Chef's Table is ₹900 you did not plan to give away.
   - **Flat amount off** → **Amount off (₹)**.
   - **Free shipping** → no value at all. It zeroes the shipping charge and only applies to shipped items.
7. **Minimum order (₹, optional)** — hint *"No minimum"*.
8. **Applies to** — tick **Prepared food**, **Packaged**, **Experience** and/or **Merchandise**. *"Leave every box clear to apply the discount to all four product types."*
9. **Starts** and **Ends** — both read in India Standard Time whatever your laptop is set to.
10. **Total redemptions (optional)** and **Per customer (optional)** — both default to unlimited.
11. Click **Create coupon**.

## Reading the table

Columns: **Code**, **Type**, **Value**, **Min order**, **Max discount**, **Applies to**, **Window**, **Usage**, **Per customer**, **Status**.

The badge in **Status** tells you what is *actually* happening right now, which is not always what you set:

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Live** | Working at checkout now |
| **Scheduled** | Set to Active, but the start date has not arrived |
| **Expired** | Set to Active, but the end date has passed |
| **Draft** | *"Not offered to anyone until it is set to Active"* |
| **Disabled** | *"Switched off. Redemption history is kept."* |

Empty state: **"No coupons yet"** — *"Create one to offer a discount at checkout. Coupons start as drafts, so nothing goes live until you say so."*

## Turning a coupon off

**You disable a coupon, you do not delete it.** Click the ban icon. The dialog explains why:

> *"The code stops working at checkout immediately. It stays on this list as Disabled, and every redemption already made against it is kept — a redeemed coupon is part of an order's financial record and is never deleted. You can set it back to Active later."*

Click **Disable coupon**. You will see the code confirmed back to you: `WELCOME10 disabled.`

# 🌐 How the storefront shows your work

This is the short tour that makes the whole role make sense. Every field you filled in above has a place it lands on a page a customer reads.

## Your edit → the live page

| You changed | Where a customer sees it |
| --- | --- |
| Product **Name** | The big heading at the top of the product page, and the menu card |
| Product **Slug** | The web address itself — `www.konma.store/p/konma-signature-thali` |
| Product **Description** | The paragraph under the name, **and** the Google result, **and** the WhatsApp share preview |
| Product **Type** | The small badge above the name — **Prepared food**, **Packaged**, **Experience** or **Merchandise** |
| Product **Category** | The breadcrumb trail, and its link |
| **First photo** | The big image at the top, the menu card, and the share preview |
| Photo **Alt text** | Read aloud to screen-reader users, read by Google, used in the share card |
| **Published** switch | Whether the page exists for the public at all |
| Product **Variants** | The size or option picker in the buy box |
| Experience **Description** | The copy on the experience page |
| Experience **Date**, **Capacity**, **Price** | The booking panel and the seats-left count |

## The three public pages to know

**1. A product page — `https://www.konma.store/p/<slug>`**

Top to bottom, a customer sees: a breadcrumb (**Home › Shop › Signature Plates › Konma Signature Thali**), your photos in a gallery, a type badge, the product name as a heading, a star rating if it has reviews, **your description**, the buy box with the variant picker, then the product details and the **Reviews** section.

**2. The experiences list — `https://www.konma.store/experiences`**

Heading **Experiences**, then: *"Dinners, workshops and tastings run at the villa on a fixed date, for a fixed number of people. Places go into your cart and are held for fifteen minutes once you reach checkout."*

Below that, **Upcoming** — *"Every sitting still open, soonest first."* — and further down **Past sittings** — *"These have already run. They are here because we usually run them again."*

If nothing is on sale: **"No experiences on sale just now"**.

**3. One experience — `https://www.konma.store/experiences/<slug>`**

For example `https://www.konma.store/experiences/fermentation-workshop`. Your title, your description, the date, the price and the booking panel.

> 💡 **The slug in an experience URL is the *product's* slug, not the event's title.** The Fermentation Workshop event is booked through a product called Fermentation Workshop, and it is that product's Slug field — `fermentation-workshop` — that becomes the address.

> ⚠️ **Open the live page after every meaningful edit.** It is the only way to know what you actually shipped. Two minutes of looking has saved more bad copy than any review process.

# ✅ Worked example — polish a product and watch it go live

Do this once end to end. It takes about twenty minutes and it teaches the whole role.

**The goal:** take one seeded product from thin to finished, then look at it on the real site.

- [ ] **1. Pick your product.** Sidebar → **Catalog**. On the **Konma Food** tab, open the **Pantry & Provisions** category and find **Sourdough Starter Kit**. Notice whether its card says **"No images"** — that tells you how much work it needs.

- [ ] **2. Read what is there now.** Click **Edit**, stay on the **Details** tab, and read the **Description**. It currently says: *"A live culture, feed flour, a jar and a feeding card. Everything to bake by Sunday."*

- [ ] **3. Improve the copy.** Rewrite the Description so it does two things the current one does not: name a sense, and remove any doubt. For example:
  > `A living, bubbling culture with rye feed flour, a weighted jar and a feeding card. Keep it on the counter, feed it Friday, bake by Sunday. No prior baking required.`

  Keep it to two short sentences. Do **not** touch the **Slug** — the address `sourdough-starter-kit` already exists and changing it breaks any link anyone has shared.

- [ ] **4. Save and confirm.** Click **Save Changes** and check you see **"Product updated."**

- [ ] **5. Add a photo.** Reopen the product and click the **Media** tab.
  - Click **Choose image** and pick a JPEG, PNG or WebP under 10 MB.
  - In **Alt text (required)**, write what is actually in the frame — for example `Sourdough starter kit box with jar, rye flour and feeding card`. Ten words, no "photo of".
  - Click **Upload image**. Watch it move through *"Requesting an upload slot..."*, *"Uploading..."*, *"Linking it to the product..."*, then **"Image added."**

- [ ] **6. Make sure the right photo leads.** Look at the list. The top row carries the **Cover & OG image** badge. If your new photo is not top, drag it up or use the up arrow, and check you see **"Gallery order saved."**

- [ ] **7. Make sure it is published.** Back on the menu grid, check the **Published** switch on the card is on. If you flick it, you will see **"Product published."**

- [ ] **8. Open the live page.** In a new browser tab go to:
  > **https://www.konma.store/p/sourdough-starter-kit**

  Check, one by one:
  - Your photo is the big one at the top.
  - The heading reads **Sourdough Starter Kit**.
  - **Your new description** is the paragraph underneath it.
  - The badge above the name says **Packaged**.
  - The breadcrumb reads **Home › Shop › Pantry & Provisions › Sourdough Starter Kit**.

- [ ] **9. Check the share preview.** Paste that same URL into a WhatsApp message to yourself and wait for the card to appear. The picture is your cover image, the title is the product name, the text underneath is your description. **That card is the first thing most people ever see of this product** — if it looks wrong, go back to step 3.

- [ ] **10. Now check the experience side.** Sidebar → **Experiences**. Find **Fermentation Workshop** in the table and confirm its **Type** badge reads **Workshop**, its **Status** reads **Upcoming**, and its **Capacity** shows out of **16**.

- [ ] **11. Open the live experience page.**
  > **https://www.konma.store/experiences/fermentation-workshop**

  You should see the title, the description *"A hands-on afternoon on koji, kanji and sourdough. Take your starter home."*, the date, ₹2,500, and a booking panel.

- [ ] **12. And the list it sits on.**
  > **https://www.konma.store/experiences**

  Your workshop should be under **Upcoming** — *"Every sitting still open, soonest first."*

- [ ] **13. Write down what you would change.** You have now seen exactly what a guest sees. Note the one thing that would most improve it — usually a better photograph — and make that your next task.
